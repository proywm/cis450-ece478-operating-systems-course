Description and outcomes

Prerequisites: CIS 310 and one of CIS 350/3501, IMSE 350, or ECE 370 with ECE/MATH 276; previous or concurrent IMSE 317. Verify your own degree and program requirements in the catalog. You are expected to arrive able to read and write C, use pointers and structs, compile and debug a multi-file program from a command line, and reason about binary and hexadecimal values. This course does not reteach C. If any of that is rusty, repair it in the first two weeks and use office hours early.

This course studies the operating system as a manager of CPU, memory, files, disks, and devices. Topics include processes, threads, scheduling, virtual memory, synchronization, deadlock, I/O, and file systems, with hands-on C and teaching-OS work.

  1. Explain OS abstractions, mechanisms, and policies.
  2. Analyze scheduling, translation, replacement, and file-system traces.
  3. Identify races, invariants, synchronization requirements, and liveness risks.
  4. Build, modify, test, and explain basic components in a reproducible environment.
  5. Communicate design decisions, evidence, limitations, and contributions.

Required material and technology

Operating Systems: Three Easy Pieces (OSTEP), free online. Students also need a POSIX build environment providing C, pthreads, Make, Python 3, GDB, and QEMU. Linux and macOS students may use a native toolchain. Windows students, and anyone whose native toolchain will not build the course sources, should use Docker Desktop with the container recipe given in the Programming 1A prompt. Programming assignments use the official MIT x86 xv6-public repository at commit eeb7b415dbcb12cc362d0783e41c3d1f44066b17; each Canvas assignment states the revision it expects. Install and verify this environment during the first two weeks. A setup problem discovered near a deadline is not grounds for an extension.

Learning environment and accessibility

Each module combines a mapped OSTEP reading, an accessible text explanation, readiness questions with worked answers, and hands-on work. The readiness questions are ungraded self-checks; nothing you do on a module page is recorded or reported. Course HTML uses semantic structure, keyboard access, visible focus, reflow, and text alternatives. Report access barriers promptly.

Support routes for recurring barriers

  • Build the environment in week one, not the night before Programming 1A is due. Confirm C, pthreads, Make, Python 3, GDB, and QEMU all run before assessed work begins.
  • Apple-silicon and other non-x86 hosts run the historical x86 kernel under linux/amd64 emulation. Use the headless make qemu-nox route rather than a graphical QEMU window.
  • Clone xv6 fresh from the official repository at the stated commit rather than reusing a downloaded archive or a nested extraction.
  • Before xv6 or pthread work, state the invariant and the trace you expect, then compare it against what the program actually does. A disagreement is the finding, not a failure.
  • Follow the current Canvas rubric for required evidence, then reopen the submission receipt and the uploaded files. A local folder, a screenshot, or a test that passes on your machine is not a Canvas submission.

Deadlines, late work, and missed assessments

This course has no GSI or grader. Every submission is read by the instructor, so the rules below are applied uniformly and without individual negotiation. Plan your schedule around them.

Late work

  • Homework and programming assignments are accepted up to 48 hours after the Canvas deadline with a flat 20% deduction from the earned score. After 48 hours Canvas closes the submission and the score is zero.
  • Participation quizzes and in-class evidence checks are completed during the class meeting in which they are assigned. They are not accepted late and are not offered individually afterward. The two lowest scores in this category are dropped instead.
  • Examinations are taken at the scheduled time. There is no late window.
  • The 48-hour window is the extension. Requests for additional time, including requests made before the deadline, are declined so that the same rule applies to everyone.

Reasons that do not extend a deadline

Submit early enough that ordinary problems do not cost you the deadline. The following are foreseeable and do not justify late credit: a build or container that stops working, a laptop failure, a full disk, lost or unsaved work, an expired cloud session, uploading the wrong file, a Canvas submission you did not verify, heavy workload in other courses, travel, and interview or work schedules. Keep your work in version control and submit a complete draft well before the deadline; you may resubmit until the deadline closes.

Missed examinations

Make-up examinations are not given. If you miss the midterm for a reason the University requires be accommodated — a documented illness or emergency, a religious observance, a University-sponsored activity, or an approved accessibility accommodation — notify the instructor in writing before the examination whenever the circumstance is foreseeable, and as soon as possible otherwise. In an approved case the midterm weight is transferred to the final examination rather than rescheduled. A missed midterm without an approved reason receives a zero. The final examination is given only at the Registrar's scheduled time; a conflict with that time must be raised with the instructor and the Registrar well in advance.

Accommodations and documentation

Approved accessibility accommodations always take precedence over the rules on this page. Register with Disability and Accessibility Services and share your accommodation letter with the instructor early in the term, not at the first deadline you need it for. Do not email medical documentation; ask where it should be sent.

Grades and regrades

Check every returned score in Canvas. A regrade request must be submitted in writing within one week of the grade being posted, must identify the specific rubric criterion in dispute, and must explain what the submitted evidence shows. The whole submission is re-evaluated, so a score can move in either direction. Requests after one week, and requests to review the course grade at the end of the term, are not considered.

Academic integrity and AI

University rules and each Canvas assignment's policy apply. Unless explicitly authorized, do not use AI to produce submitted code, calculations, explanations, or reports. Do not upload private course solutions or student data. Appropriate support includes concept explanations, smaller analogous examples, error interpretation, and feedback on reasoning you wrote. You remain responsible for every line you submit and must be able to explain it on request.

Attendance, accommodations, and respectful learning

Regular participation is expected; consult Canvas for the active mechanism and notify the instructor about circumstances affecting attendance. Students seeking accommodations should register with Disability and Accessibility Services and notify the instructor. Questions and partial reasoning should be handled respectfully and professionally. University incident-reporting resources are available through UM-Dearborn.
